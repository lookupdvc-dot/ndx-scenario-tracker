A 2022-style grind lower is the base case, with the low still ahead

The NDX closed below its 6 October record. The base case is a relief rally that fails near the 200-day, then a second leg to 19,400–20,100 around Q2 2027. That path needs earnings estimates to be cut, and so far they are still rising, so the recovery case carries 35%. No US trading since Friday: futures point to a bounce on Monday, and the week's tests are Wednesday's PCE inflation and Nvidia's 25 November Treasury deadline.

What the valuations say

Read: supportive, with a catch. A 20% price fall with rising earnings estimates has taken the forward multiple from about 23× at the end of September to 17.1×, the lowest since late 2022. Its premium to the S&P 500 (17.0× on LSEG estimates) has all but gone, against a ten-year average of about 20%. This is nothing like 2000, when the index traded on more than 100× trailing earnings.

The catch is the "E". The multiple is only cheap if AI earnings hold. Semiconductor profits are cyclical, and a low P/E on peak earnings is the classic trap. If hyperscalers cut 2027 capex, forward earnings fall and the same price looks expensive again.

Bonds compete. The index's earnings yield (about 5.9%) is roughly one point above the 10-year Treasury (4.95%). That gap has reopened in the sell-off but is still thinner than the 1.8 points at the end of 2021.

      Fundamental read

      Earnings are strong and the index now trades on 17.1× forward earnings, which argues against a 2000-style collapse and means much of the base-case fall would need earnings estimates to be cut, not just multiples to fall. But the three things that set the multiple are all unfavourable or uncertain: the Fed is hiking, state-level policy threatens the data-centre build-out, and 2027 capex guidance does not arrive until late January. Analysts are still raising estimates and no hyperscaler has cut capex, yet half of fund managers expect a cut within six months. Over the weekend the policy news got worse (a threatened tax on overseas data centres, a wider Congressional inquiry into Nvidia, brokers adding Fed hikes) while the estimate data did not. The market is pricing an earnings downgrade that has not happened. If it arrives, the base case plays out; if it does not, the recovery case does.
